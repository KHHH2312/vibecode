# Kaggriculture — 22 Sep 2026: the game is a photo finish, and the market is one shared pool

This supersedes the strategy sections of `handoff.md`. That document's method
warnings (the 256-Elo ladder noise floor, single-grid results lying,
non-comparable submission ages) all still hold and should still be read first.

that is not decided by strategy.

## 3. What the engine actually is

Read `kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py` — 1,072 lines,
and the whole economy is in it.

**One shared market.** There is a single `market["inventory"]` dict for both
players. Price is

```
price(inv) = base ± (target * base / f(T)) * f(|inv - I0|)      I0 = 10000
```

with per-good `f` and `target`:

| good | base | T | glut curve | glut target | scarcity curve | scarcity target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MELON | 250 | 300 | **sq** | 3.60 | log | 0.20 |
| WOOL | 200 | 105 | **sq** | 3.20 | log | 0.20 |
| MILK | 160 | 122 | linear | 1.60 | sqrt | 0.60 |
| STRAWBERRY | 120 | 100 | linear | 1.60 | sqrt | 0.70 |
| FERTILIZER | 100 | 200 | linear | 0.40 | linear | 0.40 |
| TOMATO | 60 | 200 | sqrt | 0.60 | linear | 0.40 |
| EGG | 50 | 332 | log | 0.20 | linear | 0.40 |
| CARROT | 35 | 450 | sqrt | 0.70 | log | 0.20 |
| WHEAT | 25 | 400 | log | 0.20 | sqrt | 0.80 |

WOOL loses its entire value over ~58 units of glut and MELON over ~158, while
WHEAT and EGG barely move at all. The first units sold are worth several times
the last.

**Demand is finite and shared.** Inventory only falls through town consumption:
every 4 steps each unlocked shop removes 1 unit of each of its products (2 if
the shop sells only one), and every 12 steps the town centre removes 1/2/4
units (days 0–9 / 10–19 / 20–29) of every product **except FERTILIZER**. So

* town centre alone is 140 units per good over a season;
* **MELON appears in no shop** — 140 units of demand for the whole game, split
  between both players;
* **FERTILIZER has no demand at all.** Its inventory never falls. Selling it is
  a one-way, non-recovering race.

**Orders resolve in lockstep by slot index.** `_process_market` walks order
index `i` for both players together and quotes both against the same
pre-commit inventory. There is no seat bias — a mirror game ends in an exact
tie — but a good sold at a *lower slot index*, or on an earlier turn, is sold
before the opponent's units push the price down.

**Other facts worth having:**

* Reward is `farms[player]["money"]` at the end. Shed, seeds, animals and land
  are scored at nothing.
* DONE fires at `step >= episodeSteps - 2`, so **step 718 is the last turn
  whose actions are executed**.
* `maxMarketOrdersPerTurn = 10`; everything past the tenth order is discarded.
* Hiring costs `fib(n)` for the n-th hire *of that day* with the multiplier at
  1 — ten hands in a day costs 143 — and **nothing caps the number of hands**.
  Hands and `hires_today` reset every night.
* Land is three extra quadrants at 1000 / 2000 / 4000. Top agents buy three and
  leave the fourth locked.
* FEED consumes **one WHEAT per animal per day, from the hand's own
  inventory**, not the shed. Two consecutive unfed days and the animal escapes,
  leaving the empty structure. The care bonus that doubles a production day is
  only granted on a day the animal was both fed *and* cared for.
* A buy/sell round trip on an unchanged market nets exactly zero — BUY_PRODUCT
  is quoted at `inventory - 1` on purpose. There is no arbitrage.

## 6. What works

### The race-dump layer — **+642 ± 79 coins, 38W-2L-0T**

`mkdump.py`. Appends SELL orders for goods sitting in the shed into the free
slots below whatever the parent already issued, never displacing a parent
order. Tested as `FROM=240 FRAC=0.80 CAP=6 ITEMS=WOOL,MELON,STRAWBERRY,MILK`
on the V61 base. Submitted as **V63-RACEDUMP**.

Held stock is stock the opponent sells ahead of us into a shared pool with
quadratic glut curves. Selling it now also takes the top of the curve away from
them, and gets cash into hand early enough to buy the animals we were missing.

**This is the same idea as the "surplus sell layer" recorded as a failure.** It
failed because it was measured by replaying one seat against a fixed tape,
which scores only our own cash and cannot see the value a sale takes *from* the
other player. Head to head it is one of the largest effects found in the whole
project. Any experiment about the shared market must be run head to head.

### Things measured and rejected

| change | result vs V61 |
|---|---|
| V62 unconditional SELL→BUY→HIRE reorder | −42 ± 18, 26/40 games identical — cancels V61's gain exactly |
| Sell-priority sort by price impact (steepest first) | **0W-40L**, −516 ± 51 — the parent's sell order is already better |
| Our old v43/V39 family (v57, LOOK_HI=44) | −1,732 ± 359 (8W-32L) — abandon that lineage |
| Terminal frugality (block seed/animal/land buys that cannot mature) | no-op: `seedlog.py` shows the V61 base makes **zero** purchases after day 18 |

## 7. Running things

Everything lives in the session scratchpad with `./venv/bin/python`. **The box
has four cores — never run more than one `ab.py` at a time, and use
`AB_WORKERS=3`.**

```
dlsubs.py 11                    # pull our own submission sources into subs/
mon.py 6                        # every live submission: Elo, games, win%, drift, verdict
gap.py <sub> [<sub> ...]        # margin distribution by opponent rating -> gap.json
crawl2.py <sub> ...             # walk the ladder graph for the top submissions -> ladder2.json
topcensus.py <sub> [n]          # a top agent's farm, day by day, from a public replay
epcensus.py <episode_id>        # both seats of one episode
sellcmp.py <episode_id>         # both seats' sell ledgers, per good and per day
revenue.py a/main.py b/main.py  # exact per-good revenue and spend for one local game
leak.py a b [seed] [cut]        # endgame: shed at the bell, spending after a cut
seedlog.py a b                  # every non-sell purchase by game day
census.py a b [seed] [seat]     # our own farm, day by day
ab.py A/main.py B/main.py 20    # head to head, both seat orders, 40 games
mkdump.py / mkfeed.py / mkfrugal.py / mkfront.py   # layer builders, MK_SRC=<base>/main.py
submit.sh <dir> "<message>"     # tar main.py and submit
```

A layer builder appends a wrapper to a copy of the base and leaves
`kaggle_submission_agent = agent` last. `kaggle_environments` runs the **last
callable in the module**, so nothing may be appended after the wrapper's `def`.

## 8. Where to go next

1. **Fill the empty pastures.** Three idle pens and three missing cows are
   worth roughly 8,000 coins a game and they compound. A market layer cannot
   do it: `BUY_ANIMAL` only puts the animal in the shed, and placing it needs a
   hand to PICKUP, walk to the pen and PLACE. It needs a hands layer, or the
   early cash that lets the existing controller buy them on time — which is
   part of what the race-dump layer provides.
2. **Meter wool.** It realises 104 against a base of 200 while YARN_STORE alone
   absorbs 2 units every 4 steps. Whether metering beats racing is an open
   question and must be settled head to head.
3. **Labour is nearly free and uncapped.** Ten hands a day costs 143 coins and
   the top agents run 11–12. If the board has unworked tiles, extra hands are
   the cheapest production on offer.
4. Keep every comparison head to head over both seat orders. Single-seat tape
   benchmarks have now produced a false negative (surplus selling) as well as
   the four false positives already recorded in `handoff.md`.

### Three hypotheses killed cleanly

**Purchase funding (`mkprec.py`) — 0W-0L-40T, exactly +0.** The theory was that
`_commit_unit` silently drops a BUY_ANIMAL the farm cannot afford, and that our
9-vs-12 cow shortfall in replay 111711110 came from 400-coin cows failing
against a 203-coin purse. `failbuy.py` patches the commit path and counts what
the engine actually drops: across a whole game, **one** wheat purchase on day 1.
Nothing else fails. The cow gap is a *plan* difference between builds, not a
funding failure, and the layer never fires.

**The `_v219` tomato expansion — 0W-0L-40T, exactly +0.** Raising
`CROP_MIN_PRICE` out of reach changes nothing on this seed grid: 40 of 40 games
byte-identical. It does fire occasionally on the ladder (replay 111866420 shows
it buying the 4,000-coin fourth quadrant on day 18 and leaving 15 tiles empty),
but it is too rare to measure here and the "+7,616, KEEP" verdict came from the
single-seat ablation method that has already produced one confirmed false
negative.

**Feed reserve (`mkfeed.py`) — 0W-40L-0T, −26,368 ± 915.** The theory came from
the sell ledger of replay 111711110: we sold 57 more wheat than the opponent
and made 99 less milk, and wheat is feed. Wrong causality. The V61 base's
animals are never short — FEED draws from the hand's inventory, not the shed,
and the hands keep up. Wheat sales are 16,623 coins of real revenue, so holding
them back deletes that revenue and leaves worthless stock at the bell. The
opponent in that replay simply ran more cows and therefore needed more of its
own wheat.

A follow-on theory — that a full shed destroys the day's harvest — is also
false for this base. `overflow.py` counts the units lost when
`_drop_inventories_to_shed` cannot fit a hand's carry: **about six units in an
entire game**, and no purchase is ever refused for a full shed, even though the
shed does touch its 100-unit cap late on. Shed pressure is real but it is not
where the money is.

### The tape still cannot be edited

**Sheep → cow swap (`mkswap.py`) — 0W-40L-0T, −135,807 ± 2,003.** Cows return
about 5,100 a head against a sheep's 2,300 and cost 400 against 500, so
rewriting `BUY_ANIMAL SHEEP` into `BUY_ANIMAL COW` looks free. It is not: the
controller then issues `PICKUP SHEEP` and `PLACE SHEEP`, finds no sheep, and
the animal is stranded in the shed while the routine unravels. This is the
cleanest demonstration yet of `handoff.md` §5.A — a wrapper may reorder, filter
or append market orders, but it may not change what the parent asked for.

| 0 | 51.6% | +11 | ~2,660 |
| +642 | 87.5% | +338 | ~2,990 |

## 10. The town consumption clock — the best layer found

`_town_consume` runs **after** `_process_market` in the same step, and each
unlocked shop removes its products from inventory every 4 steps. So the market
inventory is at its lowest — and the price at its highest — at the start of the
step immediately after a shop tick, i.e. on steps where `step % 4 == 1`.

Firing the dump layer only on those steps, changing nothing else, is worth
another ~43 coins a game over firing every turn:

| variant | firing rule | result |
|---|---|---|
| **tk4** | `step % 4 == 1`, gate 0.90, cap 6, day 10+ | **40W-0L-0T, +759 ± 89** |
| rd6 | every step, otherwise identical | 40W-0L-0T, +716 ± 80 |

The obvious objection is that this is not the clock at all — a layer that fires
on one turn in four simply sells in a gentler trickle. The phase sweep settles
it. All four remainders fire equally often, so only the phase differs:

| phase | result |
|---|---|
| `% 4 == 0` (on the tick) | 38W-2L, +538 ± 71 |
| **`% 4 == 1` (just after)** | **40W-0L, +759 ± 89** |
| `% 4 == 2` | 40W-0L, +636 ± 85 |
| `% 4 == 3` | 38W-2L, +559 ± 92 |

A clean peak one step after the tick, decaying as the next tick approaches.
And the period matters as much as the phase — 4 is the shops' own interval:

| period | result |
|---|---|
| every step | 40W-0L, +716 ± 80 |
| `% 2 == 1` | 38W-2L, +736 ± 81 |
| `% 3 == 1` | 38W-0L-2T, +658 ± 88 |
| **`% 4 == 1`** | **40W-0L, +759 ± 89** |
| `% 12 == 1` | 38W-2L, +499 ± 55 |

Read this column carefully: **mod 2 and mod 4 are not separated by this data**
(+736 ± 81 against +759 ± 89). Only the two extremes are clearly worse. So the
*period* is weak evidence on its own and the honest claim is narrower than
"the peak sits at the consumption interval".

The **phase** sweep is the solid part. Those four runs fire equally often, so
trickle rate is held constant and only the offset changes, and they give a
clean peak one step after the tick with a 221-coin spread. That cannot be a
firing-rate effect. Mod 4 remainder 1 is the best measured point and is what
V64 ships, but the mechanism is established by the phase table above, not by
the period table here.

### Two more failures worth keeping

**Dumping wheat — 0W-40L, −16,853.** Together with the feed-reserve result
(−26,368 for withholding wheat) this pins the tape's wheat schedule from both
sides: selling less is worse, selling more is worse. FEED draws from the hand's
inventory, which the hands refill from the shed, so dumping shed wheat starves
the pickups; withholding it deletes 16,623 coins of real revenue. The schedule
is already at its optimum and neither direction is available.

**The shed-room layer — RETRACTED, never actually tested.** It was recorded
here as 0W-40L, −188,743. That number is not a measurement of the idea: the
builder named its layer global `_SR_PARENT`, and **the V61 base already uses
that name at line 5142**, so the inner layer called itself and the agent died
with `RecursionError` on its first turn. A forfeit scores like a real and
catastrophic result. Three stacked per-item layers failed the same way, all
four reporting the identical figure to the digit — which is what gave it away,
since four different ideas cannot fail identically.

Every layer builder now derives its globals from a prefix checked against the
parent source (`_unique_prefix`), and both `worker2.sh` and `submit.sh` run
`smoke.py` first, which plays a few turns and refuses a build that logs
anything on stderr. **Check an implausibly large negative the same way: if two
builds report the same number, neither is a measurement.**

The submitted builds are unaffected — `_RD_PARENT` appears nowhere in the base,
and V63/V64 smoke-test clean.

## 11. What the layer is actually doing — and what it is not

The line of work started from a slot-index argument: `_process_market` walks
order index `i` for both players together, so a sell at a lower index is quoted
before the opponent's sell of the same good at a higher one. That argument is
true about the engine but **it is not the mechanism**, and the test that shows
it is cheap.

`tk4pre` is tk4 with its dump orders *prepended* instead of appended — same
volume, same timing, same goods, strictly lower slot indices:

| build | order placement | result |
|---|---|---|
| **tk4** | appended below the parent's orders | **40W-0L, +759 ± 89** |
| tk4pre | prepended above them | 26W-14L, +314 ± 86 |

Prepending loses 445 coins. If winning the per-slot race were what paid, it
would gain. It is the second time the parent's own ordering has beaten a
substitute — `fr1`, which sorted the parent's sells by price impact, lost 516.

So the mechanism is the plainer one: **the layer turns idle shed stock into
cash earlier in the game, at the moments the price is highest.** The
consumption-clock phase result is the direct evidence for the "when" half, and
the price gate is what keeps it from selling into a glut. The shared-pool
framing still holds in the weak sense that stock we hold is stock the opponent
sells first, but nothing inside a single turn's order list is worth fighting
over.

**Rule for this codebase, now twice confirmed: append, never reorder.**

### The layer is a local optimum

Every stacked or structural variant, all against the V61 base, all smoke-tested:

| build | change from tk4 | result |
|---|---|---|
| **tk4** | — | **40W-0L, +759 ± 89** |
| st_ctr | extra STRAWBERRY/MILK pass on the centre clock | 40W-0L, +751 ± 91 |
| st_mel | extra MELON pass, gate 0.70, centre clock | 40W-0L, +759 ± 89 (exact no-op) |
| st_wool | extra WOOL pass, gate 0.50 | 36W-4L, +645 ± 109 |
| rm6 | shed-fullness trigger stacked on top | 32W-8L, +357 ± 70 |
| tk4pre | prepend instead of append | 26W-14L, +314 ± 86 |
| rm1 | shed-fullness trigger alone on the base | 30W-10L, +170 ± 74 |

`st_mel` reproducing tk4's score **to the digit** is a no-op, not a failure:
tk4's own layer has already cleared the melon before the extra pass can fire.
Distinguish the two by sign and size — a build that does not load forfeits and
reports a huge negative; a layer that never fires reproduces its parent exactly.

`st_wool` is the useful negative. Below 0.90 of base the wool book is already
glutted and its curve is quadratic, so selling more digs deeper. The main
gate shutting wool out late in the game is correct behaviour, not a missed
opportunity — and the same gate still admits wool *early*, while it is near
base, which is where rd18 (+549 with wool and melon removed, against rd6's
+716) shows the value is.

That last point corrects something stated earlier in this file: the price gate
is not a static item selector. Prices start at base and drift down as goods are
sold, so the gate admits wool and melon early and excludes them later. That is
exactly the right treatment for a steep-curve good, and it is why the four-item
list beats the two-item one. A whole-game mean price hides the early window
completely.

## 12. The homestead crew, and why self-play cannot judge a change

The public meta is one agent. Measured in self-play on a common seed grid, our
base, antigravity's build and five top public notebooks all land between
113,000 and 125,000 mean end-of-season cash, the top six within 1,300 coins of
each other. There is no stronger base to adopt, which is why everyone converges
near 2,650 and the top is decided by coin flips.

So the only way up is production nobody else has. Two engine facts open a door:

* Farm hands are uncapped and cost `fib(n)` for the n-th hire of the day, and
  the parent's hiring always finishes by **hour 4** (measured across a whole
  season), so hands hired later are ours to drive.
* The parent sizes its `hands` list to the real hand count, so extra hands are
  not invisible -- but it issues them `PASS`. Overriding the trailing entries
  takes nothing away from it.

`mkhome.py` builds on that: hire a small crew, plant the tiles the parent
leaves empty as it harvests, water, and harvest. Four real bugs came out of
making it work, each worth recording:

1. **Crew size inferred from the index gap.** The parent's declared hand count
   moves during the day, so the gap shrank and the layer re-hired -- at
   `fib(13)`, `fib(14)`, `fib(15)`: 377, 610, 987 a turn. It drained the purse
   to nothing. Count hands the farm actually holds instead.
2. **Harvesting a non-ongoing crop early destroys the tile.** WHEAT, CARROT and
   MELON start at `yield_units: 1` and gain **only from WATER**, inside the
   window `[(max_yield_day+1)//2, max_yield_day]`. Harvesting on `units > 0`
   threw a whole tile away for one unit.
3. **New plants die the night they are sown.** `_new_plant` sets
   `consecutive_unwatered: 1`, and two dry days makes a weed, so a tile must be
   watered on its planting day. Watering sat below harvesting in priority.
4. **Contention.** On shared land the crew fought the parent for tiles. Keeping
   a registry of the tiles the crew sowed, and touching nothing else, recovered
   about 3,900 coins a game.

### The result, and the measurement that overturned it

Scored on self-play mean cash, the work looked like a steady climb:

| change | self-play mean | vs tk4 (132,763) |
|---|---|---|
| buy the fourth quadrant, 2 hands, strawberry | 115,201 | −17,562 |
| fix the runaway hiring | 118,123 | −14,640 |
| drop the land purchase | 128,852 | −3,911 |
| crew works only its own tiles | 132,768 | +5 |
| hire only when tiles are free | 132,891 | +128 |
| carrot, threshold 6 | 133,402 | **+639** |

Then the paired head-to-head:

| test | result |
|---|---|
| r_c6 vs tk4, grid 900000 | **0W-40L, −6,391 ± 178** |
| r_c6 vs tk4, grid 1700000 (fresh) | **0W-40L, −6,567 ± 254** |
| self-play, 16 seeds | r_c6 130,388 vs tk4 130,547 |

**The crew loses about 6,400 coins a game, and self-play cannot see it.** In a
mirror both seats hire the crew, so its wages cancel and the two builds tie. In
a real game only one seat pays them, and the carrots it grows sell at 41 into a
market the opponent is selling into too -- nowhere near the ~4,300 of wages plus
the price impact.

**Rule: never judge a change by self-play.** Any cost both sides pay disappears
from a mirror, so a mirror will happily rank a pure liability as neutral. Use
self-play only to answer "how much money does this agent make on this board",
which is what an absolute target like 150,000 asks. Use a paired head-to-head,
both seat orders, on a grid the build was not tuned on, to answer "is this
change an improvement". Every one of the gains in the table above is an
artifact of the wrong metric.

### What 150,000 actually is

Across 537 real ladder games our own final cash averages 99,628, median 98,922,
p90 129,797, max 187,863. Games reaching 150,000: **11 of 537 (2.0%)** for us,
and **6 of 537 (1.1%)** for our opponents, a set that includes agents rated
above 3,100. Clearing 150,000 is a property of a rich shop draw, not of agent
skill, and no agent in this competition achieves it as a floor. The meaningful
targets are mean cash and win rate.

## 13. A layer can be in the file and not in the game

Six parameter variants of the dump layer were scored against tk4 on a fresh
grid. Five came back with the *same line*: 6W-34L-0T, mean −441 ± 52. Two of
those five, `z_c5` and `z_c7`, differ by exactly one character in one line —
the sell cap, 5 against 7 — and they beat tk4 in the same six games and lost
the same thirty-four, to the coin.

Different agents do not score identically. The same signature had appeared
before, when four unrelated builds all reported exactly −188,743 and the cause
was a namespace collision. So the scores were not measuring what they claimed.

`probe.py` settles this class of question directly: it plays one game and
hashes the actual stream of market orders the agent emits. Four of the five
hashed **identical to the bare base**, `ag61`. The layer was in the file and
never ran.

### Why

`kaggle_environments` chooses the entry point with

```python
[v for v in env.values() if callable(v)][-1]
```

— the last *callable value* in the module namespace, in insertion order. A
Python dict preserves the position of a key that already exists: rebinding
`agent` leaves it wherever the base first defined it, thousands of lines
earlier. But `_RD_PARENT = agent`, the line that captures the parent, is a
**new** name holding a function, so it is inserted at the end. The engine ran
`_RD_PARENT`, which is the base, and the layer was dead code.

Every earlier build escaped this by accident: the layer ended with `del agent`
before redefining it, and the delete-then-define sequence moves `agent` to the
end of the namespace. Adding a `PARENT` environment variable, so the builder
could wrap bases whose entry point is not called `agent`, replaced that line
and silently removed the protection. The edit landed *mid-sweep*: `z_g85`, built
at 18:22:07, carries `del agent` and is live; `z_c5`, built at 18:27:01, does
not and is inert. That is the whole of the split in the results.

The same defect neutered the stacked test: `pub1tk`, our layer on the order-book
notebook, scored `16W-24L, −83 ± 88` — the identical line to `pub1` alone,
because it *was* `pub1` alone.

### Fixes

The entry point is now published under a name nothing else can hold, which is
correct whatever the parent is called and however many layers are stacked:

```python
kaggle_submission_agent = agent
_RD_ENTRY = agent          # tag-renamed per layer, so stacking stays safe
```

`mkdump.py` now refuses to finish a build unless the engine's own selection
rule picks a function whose code references the layer's constants. `vsweep.sh`
additionally probes each variant against the base it was layered on and reports
`INERT` instead of a score when the two action hashes match. Of the eight
builders, only `mkdump.py` had lost its `del agent`; results from the other
seven stand.

### The lesson, which is the same one as §12

A broken build does not score like a broken build. It scores like a change that
did not help — a clean, plausible, slightly negative number with a tight error
bar. §12 was a metric that could not see the cost of a change; this is a metric
that cannot see the *absence* of the change. Both produce publishable-looking
results that are about nothing.

The defence is not more seeds. It is to confirm the treatment was applied before
measuring its effect: hash what the agent actually does, and check it differs
from the control. A benchmark can only compare two agents — it cannot tell you
they are two.

### A real result, recovered

`pub1` — the `shiiin9/your-market-list-is-an-order-book` notebook, which scores
order permutations against a price-impact model — plays tk4 level: **−83 ± 88
over 40 paired games**, a difference well inside noise. It is the only public
base found that is not clearly behind ours.
